**Selected for the live toggle**: winner = `fixed_no_overlap` (highest nDCG@10), median = `structure_aware` (middle-ranked by nDCG@10), efficient = `fixed_no_overlap` (best nDCG@10 per chunk).

**Read these numbers with the real limitation of this eval honestly**: all 5 strategies score within 0.0006 of each other on nDCG@10, and every strategy hits recall@10 = 1.0000. This is expected, not a sign the strategies are equivalent in general — the corpus has only 8 papers, so document-level retrieval (does the *right paper* show up in the top 10, out of 8 possible papers) is a low-discrimination task almost any reasonable chunking survives. This ablation is a real, honest first read on this specific 8-paper corpus, not proof these strategies would still tie on a larger one — a genuinely discriminating version of this eval needs more papers than exist in the corpus today.

`semantic` produced far more chunks (2,932) than every other strategy (541-571) at a comparable score, driven by an unverified `_SIMILARITY_DROP_THRESHOLD=0.35` starting point (see `src/ingest/chunking_strategies.py`) splitting aggressively — flagged as a real cost/quality tradeoff worth tuning, not a strategy to adopt as-is if chunk-count efficiency mattered more here.
